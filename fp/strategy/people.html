Section A · P2 · Brand Strategy

People.

Brand Therapy is for former corporate leaders building a business of their own who need a clearer way to explain its value to prospective customers.

The Former Executive
These founders want the business they lead to live up to what they know they can achieve. Positioning becomes urgent when the early response shows that the offer is hard to understand or choose.
01
Strategic position

What is being claimed.

The brand's position on this edge, in one plain sentence.

Position

People

Brand Therapy is for former corporate leaders building a business of their own who need a clearer way to explain its value to prospective customers.

Compass summary

The Former Executive

The Former Executive is several months into building a solo business. Prospective customers still struggle to understand why they should choose the offer.

02
Offer boundaries

What belongs and what does not.

The filters that keep this strategic choice operational.

Rules in

Belongs

  • Former corporate leaders who have spent at least three months building a solo business and have put an offer in front of prospective customers
  • Consultancies, agencies, and product companies, including software, operating under the founder's name or a company name
  • Founders who can see that their message is too vague to convey the value of what they have built
Rules out

Does not belong

  • Leaders staying in a company or seeking another salaried role
  • Buyers looking only for a visual refresh or outsourced social media

Tradeoffs

What this choice costs.

Cost

Focus on the period when the founder has customer responses to learn from. This leaves the pre-launch transition outside the core audience.

Cost

Prioritize problems of credibility and authority that clearer positioning can address.

Proof required

What evidence supports this.

Every public claim traces to verifiable evidence.

  • Examples that show what a buyer misunderstood and how a clearer position changed the explanation
  • Relevant client work that shows how a strategic choice carried through to how the business presents itself
  • Evidence of commercial outcomes tied to the work when verified
06
Output control

Rules for AI.

Directives any LLM must follow when producing content for this brand.

  • Use the full ideal client profile below for content and asset work.
  • Lead with the founder's current business and what prospective customers are seeing.
  • Keep the former corporate role as career context.
  • Treat a dated first-person account as a signal to investigate and preserve uncertainty about intent.
  • Use plain language and address the founder as an accomplished peer.
07
People

Ideal client profile.

1. Who they are

Former corporate leaders who have spent at least three months building a business of their own.

2. What they’re building

They are building a business as a solo founder. The business can be a consultancy, an agency, or a product company, including software. They may operate under their own name or under a company name.

3. How far along they are

They’ve started putting the offer in front of prospective customers. The attention and response are weaker than they expected, and they can see that the message is too vague to convey the value of what they’ve built.

4. Primary pain point

At least three months into building their business, they’ve begun putting their offer in front of potential customers and are seeing less interest than they expected. Their message is too vague to give a prospect confidence that the business can deliver (credibility) or a clear reason to turn to it for a specific problem (authority). The business is also still largely unknown to the people it’s trying to reach (visibility).

The stakes are personal. They want the business they’ve chosen to build to live up to what they know they’re capable of. Its success matters to what they can achieve in the next chapter of their career and life.

5. What they want

They want to build a profitable business of their own and take pride in what it achieves. After years of contributing to other companies’ success, they want to decide where to apply their experience and see how far they can take a venture they lead. For them, success means realizing their full potential through the business they build and the impact it has.

What they need

They need a clear position in the market that gives customers a reason to choose them. Their brand must carry evidence that the business can deliver and establish the founder’s authority on the problems it solves. That message needs to reach the customers who stand to benefit from what they offer.

6. Professional identity and values

They see themselves as leaders building a business on their own terms. They value the independence to choose what to build, whom to serve, and what standards to hold themselves to. They want their reputation to reflect the quality of their judgment and what they deliver.

Self-promotion can feel uncomfortable. They’re willing to show up publicly as founders when they see how it helps the business.

7. Why it matters now

Positioning becomes a priority when a slow start turns into a pattern. After months of building the business and beginning to bring the offer to market, they can see that prospective customers still struggle to understand why they should choose it. The cost is becoming tangible. They’re spending time pursuing opportunities that rarely turn into sales, and the business is taking longer to establish than they expected.

8. What they’ve already tried

They’ve concentrated on developing the offer and getting the business ready to deliver it. They’ve spent relatively little time testing how to reach and win customers. Their assumption has been that a strong product or service will attract demand once it’s available.

The early response is forcing a decision they haven’t had to make in the same way before. They need to position what they’ve built so the right customers find it, understand its value, and choose it.

Buyer misconceptions
  • “Build it and they will come.” They expect a strong offer to generate its own demand once it’s available.
  • “The work speaks for itself.” They expect prospects who encounter the work to see why it matters to them and trust the business to deliver it.

9. What makes them hesitate

They can see several plausible directions and are unsure which one to commit to or what to do next. Choosing what to be known for can feel like closing the door on work they know they can do or committing to a way of presenting themselves that doesn’t feel like them.

Putting their name behind the business makes the risk of failure feel personal and public. They worry that if the venture falls short, people will see it as a judgment on their ability, with consequences for the reputation they’ve spent years building. They may keep refining the offer, product, or website because those changes are within their control and putting the business in front of people means facing a response they can’t control.

10. What earns their trust

They’ve spent months making decisions on their own. They start to trust Florian when he can see why they’re stuck, make a clear call on what the business should be known for, and explain why that direction fits what they’re trying to build.

His own brand and relevant client work show them whether he can carry a strategic choice through to the way a business looks, sounds, and presents itself to customers. They want to feel confident putting him in charge of that work and proud to put their name behind the result.

11. When they’re ready to buy

They start looking for answers when the business gets less response than they expected. They may be trying to explain what they’ve built, attract customers, or understand why their message falls flat. They’re ready to hire when Florian can show how positioning is affecting a goal they care about, they want to address it now, and they trust him to lead the work.

How to find these founders

Look for a dated move from senior corporate leadership into a solo venture. Use LinkedIn career histories, founder announcements, and business websites to establish the former role and function, when they began building, and what they’ve taken to market. Record the venture type, market, and working language when those are public. Leave uncertain facts open.

Give priority to founders who describe a recent problem in their own words. A prospect misunderstood the offer, compared it with a more familiar service, or couldn’t see why to choose it. The signal is stronger when the founder is already revising the pitch, narrowing the offer, or looking for help with positioning. Capture their words, the date, and what they did next. That gives content and campaign teams the language to use and a reason to reach out when the problem is current.
