Your Strategy

Close the gap between thinking and impact

Positioning statement

Heidi helps capable VPs and emerging executives close the gap between the quality of their thinking and the impact of their communication.

The work develops executive communication judgment. Scripts, slide polish, and a manufactured executive persona stay out. The leader leaves able to find the real point, lead with it, and stay grounded when challenged.

Category
Executive communication coach, Exponential Impact
Distinctive idea
Leading with intention
Founder role
Heidi is the brand. Exponential Impact is the vehicle. Content speaks as Heidi, one person, in the room with one leader

The Focus Star

The whole brand in five lines

  1. Product Close the gap between thinking and impact

    The client pays for executive communication judgment in high-stakes rooms. Scripts, slide polish, and a manufactured persona stay out.

  2. People Capable VPs whose communication has to catch up with the job

    Persona: The VP whose thinking is stronger than how it lands. Respected, trusted, now expected to think across the business. He leaves a meeting knowing what he meant, and seeing that it did not land.

  3. Purpose Steward influence so one leader's growth multiplies

    Influence is already there. The work is to use it well, so the change does not stop with the person in the room.

  4. Promise Leading with intention

    Brand DNA: Leading with intention. Clarity is how the work happens. Influence you already have, used on purpose instead of by default.

  5. Personality Warm, grounded, sharp, and substantial

    She gets it. She will not waste the hour. She will make him think. Warmth without softness around the truth. Confidence without ego. Forward movement, not six months admiring the problem.

Your Identity

Warm, grounded, sharp, substantial

Personality

One character on every channel

Warm, grounded, sharp, and substantial. She gets it, she will not waste the hour, and she will make him think.

This is the character every post, photo, and reply keeps. A VP should feel he could tell Heidi what is actually going on, and also that she will ask for the recommendation underneath the explanation.

In the roomWarmth with an edge of truth

Genuinely interested, quickly under the surface of the story, willing to challenge. He feels seen, and he leaves with something to do.

On the pageOne clear idea, and a way forward

Keep the page uncluttered, without badge walls or buzzwords. Depth without heaviness. The reader leaves knowing the three things that mattered.

In the pictureA real conversation

Seated across a table, slightly turned, engaged. Architectural rooms with warmth and texture. Bronze used in a few places. Deep ink carries the shell.

Brand archetype

The sage who stays human

The mix follows from how the brand behaves: warm, grounded, sharp, and substantial. Sage leads. Friend and Ruler carry the room. Caregiver and Innocent keep the work human. Hero is a trace of forward movement.

  1. Sage leads. Judgment in high-stakes rooms: what matters, what to say first, when to stop.
  2. Friend and Ruler carry it. Friend keeps the conversation human. Ruler holds the standard for how influence is used.
  3. Caregiver and Innocent support. Caregiver builds capacity so the leader can run the next meeting alone. Innocent keeps the honesty.
  4. Hero stays a trace. Forward movement, never a battle cry.

Outlaw, Magician, Jester, Lover, Creator, and Explorer stay at zero. Shock, theater, and magic-trick promises do not belong here.

Voice principles

Sound like the person who found the real point

01

Find the headline first

Name the business issue, then the recommendation. Context comes after, and only as much as this audience needs.

You are thinking about five things. Which one does the executive actually need?
02

Warmth with an edge of truth

Make him feel seen. Do not let him hide in the explanation. Challenge without making him defensive.

That is the explanation. What is the recommendation?
03

Clarity that changes the next move

Insight is unfinished until he knows what he will say in Tuesday's meeting.

I went in with twenty thoughts and came out knowing the three that actually mattered.
04

Sound more like himself

Skip a manufactured executive persona and skip polish for its own sake. The voice gets clearer and more confident, still his.

I sound more like myself now, just clearer and more confident.

How it looks

Ink shell, human photography, bronze used sparingly

Shell

Deep ink first screen, ivory type, warm ivory field. Bronze in a thin rule, one significant word, or a small button. Deep olive only for a point of conviction.

Photography

Genuine engagement: seated across a table, slightly turned, or standing in an architectural room with warmth and texture.

Type

editorial serif plus a clean sans, without ornate script. Baskerville for headlines, Seravek for reading type.

Leave out

Leave out arms-crossed coach portraits, badge walls, six claims on the first screen, and cluttered calls to action. The first screen holds one idea, with nothing else competing for it.

Design decisions

Why it looks this way

Every choice below answers something the work already demands. The register it adds up to is mature, calm, and luxurious.

03

Reading type

Product
What is true

Substance has to survive being read on a phone between two meetings.

What the design does

Seravek carries body copy and navigation. It holds its shape at small sizes and stays readable in long stretches.

04

The accent

Promise
What is true

Attention is the scarcest thing in the rooms this work happens in.

What the design does

Bronze takes the small details of the page: thin rules, numerals, an occasional emphasized word. It stays scarce enough to register every time it appears.

05

The reading field

Personality
What is true

Warmth is part of the work, and a screen of pure white has none of it.

What the design does

Warm ivory carries body copy instead of a sterile white, so a full page of thinking stays easy to sit with.

06

Conviction

Personality
What is true

What the design does

Deep olive marks a single point of conviction. It never runs as body text and never labels a control.

People

Real photography of genuine engagement: across a table, mid-conversation, in rooms with texture and daylight, never a staged authority pose.

Your 30-Day Plan

Own the relationship on LinkedIn

The objective

Rebuild LinkedIn around Leading with intention, start ten warm conversations, and book five first meetings from people who already know the work or fit the persona. Primary signal: five first conversations booked from LinkedIn or warm outreach in thirty days.
01

Rebuild the profile around one idea

Update the headline, the About, the banner, and the three featured pieces so the position is clear before anyone reads a post.

02

Activate people who already trust the work

Send ten short messages. Name the focus in one sentence. Ask one question. Make replying easy with the word LAND.

03

Prove the distinction in public

Three posts: the gap, the meeting that did not land, and the work that gets a no. Same voice as the About.

Four-week sequence

Do these in order

  1. Week01

    Set the position

    Update LinkedIn. Publish the first featured piece. Send the first five warm messages. Ask for real portraits from a photographer or from existing files.

    Profile + portraits
  2. Week02

    Prove it with content

    Publish one post per territory. Keep the LAND close. Reply to every comment with a genuine question instead of a pitch.

    Proof + listening
  3. Week03

    Fill the calendar

    Send the last five warm messages. Offer one short working session to a fit VP: find the headline for Tuesday's meeting.

    Outreach + service
  4. Week04

    Review what landed

    Look at the five conversations. Note the first problem each person named. Feed that language back into the next month of posts.

    Language + next month
