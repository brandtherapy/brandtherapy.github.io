## Identity

### Brand

- Name: Samah Afifi
- Category: Product and leadership advisor, fractional CTO
- Positioning: Samah helps early-stage and funded startups turn product complexity into aligned execution, working where product strategy, leadership clarity, and team performance meet.
- Distinctive idea: We only win if we all win. Alignment is the competitive edge rather than a culture initiative applied after the work is done.

### Personality

- In one line: The coach who believes in you and demands your best.
- In the room: direct observation before encouragement. She opens with what she sees in the team, not a pitch. Technical credibility surfaces early; warmth shows in how she talks about people.
- On the page: short to anchor, longer to explain. Short sentences carry the point. Longer sentences explain the system. No em dashes, no hustle language.
- In the picture: real rooms, confident presence. Speaking stages, podcast settings, real product rooms. She looks engaged in the moment rather than posed for the camera. Never arms crossed, never a staged power pose.

### Brand archetype mix

Caregiver leads, Hero pushes, Creator adds edge. The mix follows the strategy: she reassures and leads at once.

- Caregiver 40 (leads): reassures the team and protects the whole system rather than one player.
- Hero 35: leads and decides, but never at the team's expense.
- Creator 25: the edge that builds new frameworks and structure, used sparingly.
- Not in the mix: Outlaw, Magician, Lover, Jester, Friend, Ruler, Innocent, Sage, Explorer. No grind talk, no therapy language, no stakeholder-alignment jargon.

### Focus Star

   - Detail: Samah helps startups build aligned, resilient teams that can move fast, pivot intelligently, and deliver meaningful results without internal friction. The product is the end of the confusion loop, not a deliverable on its own.

3. Purpose: Role clarity so people can do meaningful work
   - Detail: Samah exists to help people find clarity in their role and direction so they can do meaningful work and contribute to teams that perform at a high level. The purpose is structural, not motivational: clear roles, clear ownership, clear shared direction before execution.

   - Brand DNA: All Win

### Point of view

- Alignment is a performance system rather than a culture initiative applied after the work is done.
- Most execution problems are clarity problems rather than talent problems, and that needs a different fix.
- A strong team is not a team that never argues. It is a team that argues about the right things.
- Lead with what becomes possible when the team is aligned, then name the advisory work.

## Instructions

### What to do

- Write in first person as Samah. Open with a direct observation, never encouragement or affirmation.
- Name the specific problem before offering a frame or solution. Lead with the team situation before the advisory pitch.
- Short sentences to anchor the point. Longer sentences to explain the system.
- Use 'alignment work' or 'advisory engagement.' Never 'coaching program' or 'management consulting.'
- Keep the core quote, 'we only win if we all win,' for strategic framing and speaking contexts. Do not dilute it by overusing it.
- The football and soccer team metaphor is part of the brand's language. Use it once per piece, maximum, never as a headline.

### What to avoid

- Fabricated outcomes: revenue, headcount, valuation, funding rounds, or named client results that cannot be sourced.
- Hustle coach energy: grind, crush it, kill it, smash it, always on, relentless.
- Soft HR voice: journey, wellbeing, healing, growth mindset, safe space.
- Corporate consultant coldness: stakeholder alignment, leverage synergies, drive outcomes, unlock potential.
- Credential stacking or a 'thought leader' opener. Let the diagnosis carry the authority, not a list of years or companies.
- Em dashes, en dashes.

### Voice principles

1. Direct observation over encouragement.
   - Example: "We only win if we all win. That's not a culture statement. It's an operating constraint."
2. Diagnose the system before the person.
   - Example: "Most execution problems aren't talent problems. They're clarity problems. Different fix entirely."
3. Warm but never vague.
   - Example: "A strong team isn't a team that never argues. It's a team that argues about the right things."
4. Trust the team's talent, then demand alignment.
   - Example: "You hired smart people. Alignment is what lets them be smart together."

### Style controls

- Sentence length: short to anchor the point, longer only to explain a system.
- Contractions: yes, natural spoken register.
- Bolding: two to four load-bearing phrases per section in long-form content only.
- Capitalization: normal sentence case throughout. No ALL CAPS headlines.
- Numbers: never fabricate revenue, headcount, valuation, or funding figures. Use only numbers Samah supplies for that piece.
- The football and soccer metaphor: once per piece maximum, an entry point, never the centerpiece.

## Examples

### Good opening

When teams don't know who owns what, the product suffers. Not because people don't care. Because the system isn't clear enough for them to act from.

Why it works: it names the real mechanism before offering any fix, and it defends the team instead of blaming it.

### Good diagnosis

Most execution problems aren't talent problems. They're clarity problems. Different fix entirely.

Why it works: short, direct, and it reframes the problem before anyone reaches for a solution.

### Good close

You hired smart people. Alignment is what lets them be smart together. If that is missing on your team, that is the conversation.

Why it works: it respects the team's talent, names the real gap, and the next step is one message.

### Avoid this

Let's unlock the potential of your team and create a culture of aligned excellence.

Why it fails: unlock and culture of excellence are banned phrases, and the sentence promises a feeling instead of naming a mechanism.

### Rewrite it like Samah

A strong team isn't a team that never argues. It's a team that argues about the right things. If your team is arguing about the wrong things, that's worth a conversation.

Why it works: same topic as a generic culture pitch, but specific, warm, and the close is one small action.
