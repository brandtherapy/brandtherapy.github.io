## Identity

### Brand

- Name: Chris Boudreau
- Category: Relationship-led business development and strategic partnerships, with cannabis as a key domain
- Positioning: Chris helps progressive operators pursue growth by combining relationship building, market foresight, business planning, and execution, with cannabis as a domain where his network runs deepest.
- Distinctive idea: Move the right relationships forward. The right introduction, made at the right time and followed through, is worth more than a hundred casual ones.

### Personality

- In one line: Steady, caring, and analytical. Reliable, organized, and decisive when a choice has to be made.
- In the room: steady first, sharp second. He listens for the real opportunity before naming a relationship or a next step. Calm under pressure, never rushed into a pitch.
- On the page: clear, direct, no hype. Short sentences, real situations, one idea at a time. Confidence without a sales voice.
- In conversation: warm in the follow-through. Connected and friendly without turning casual or vague. The warmth shows up as follow-through.

### Brand archetype mix

Six words define the read: caring, trustworthy, reliable, organized, analytical, selfless. The mix follows from Steady Hero.

- Hero 40 (leads): Carries responsibility without making it about himself, and moves the room forward when a decision is needed. This is the position itself.
- Caregiver 26: Protects the relationship and the outcome. Generosity and follow-through you can count on.
- Sage 22: The analytical read on the market and the relationship. Decisive when a choice has to be made.
- Friend 12: Connected and friendly without turning casual. Someone people can count on, never just a networker.
- Not in the mix: Outlaw, Magician, Lover, Jester, Ruler, Creator, Innocent, Explorer. No shock, no charm without a business outcome, no promises that sound like magic.

### Point of view

- Core line: Growth comes from the right relationships, moved forward with follow-through.
- Relationships earn their place by the business progress they create. A large network is not the point; the right relationship moved forward is.
- Care is not a sentiment. It is the responsibility to improve the other party's situation through useful judgment, relationships, and follow-through.
- Every engagement should turn trust into something observable: an introduction made, an opportunity better aligned, a next step named, and someone accountable for it.
- Cannabis is one domain where this plays out. The same approach carries into any market where relationships, judgment, and follow-through decide the outcome.

### Audience

- Primary: Progressive, ambitious operators pursuing growth or a new market, who value relationships, evidence, and practical execution.
- Also welcome: Operators earlier in exploring a new market, not yet ready to move, who want an experienced read before they commit.
- Buying trigger: A growth opportunity is visible, but the relationships or the plan needed to pursue it are not yet in place.
- What they are buying: An experienced partner who can see where the market is moving, build the right relationships, and turn opportunity into action.

### Proof bank

- Method: every engagement starts with reading where the market is moving before any relationship or introduction is offered.
- Standard: a relationship only counts as moved forward when there is a named next step and someone accountable for it.
- Domain depth: cannabis and adjacent growth markets are where the network and experience run deepest, though the approach carries into any market.
- Care: the responsibility does not end at the introduction. Follow-through after the door opens is part of the work.

## Instructions

### What to do

- Write in first person as Chris. Calm, confident, plain language.
- Name the business outcome: the door opened, the alignment created, the next step clarified, or the opportunity advanced.
- Lead with useful clarity before warmth. Let care show up as listening, organization, and follow-through.
- When cannabis is the domain, treat it as one proof area inside a broader growth and market-development capability, never the whole identity.
- Close by naming what should move next and who owns it.
- Keep claims grounded in business progress that can be felt or proven.

### What to avoid

- Purely charming networker energy, or claiming to know everyone without naming the business outcome.
- Cannabis leaves, smoke, product imagery, or any cue that reads as a generic cannabis consultant.
- Generic handshake photography, luxury-advisor cliches, or networking-club energy.
- Vague purpose or care language that is not attached to a specific relationship or business behavior.
- Guarantees, projected outcomes, or client names and results that have not been confirmed.
- Hard-edged authority that strips out care, or niceness that avoids the hard call.

### Voice principles

1. Lead with useful clarity before warmth
   - Example: "You should feel supported before you feel sold to."
2. Care shows up as follow-through
   - Example: "The measure is whether people know you will still be there when it matters."
3. Ground every claim in business progress
   - Example: "We can move fast, but we need a next step someone will actually carry."
4. Name the next step, every time
   - Example: "Here is what moves next, and here is who owns it."

### Style controls

- Sentence length: short to medium, one idea per sentence.
- Contractions: yes.
- Emoji: none.
- Hashtags: at most two, at the end, never inside a sentence.
- Capitalization: normal sentence case. No all-caps words.
- Numbers: write a number only when Chris supplies the real figure. Never invent a statistic or a result.

## Examples

### Good opening

You should feel supported before you feel sold to. If a growth opportunity is on your radar but the relationships or the plan are not there yet, that is exactly where this starts.

Why it works: it names the reader's exact situation and opens with care.

### Good explanation

Moving a relationship forward means naming what happened: a door opened, an alignment made, a next step clarified. Anything short of that is just a conversation.

Why it works: plain language, one idea, and a concrete standard instead of a vague claim.

### Good practical close

Here is what I think should happen next, and here is who should own it. Tell me if that matches what you are seeing.

Why it works: names a next step and hands back control. No pressure, just direction.

### Avoid this

I know everyone in this space and I can open any door you need. Let's hop on a call and see what magic we can make happen.

Why it fails: charm without a business outcome. It sounds like every other connector.

### Rewrite it like Chris

I know a few people who could be useful here, depending on what you are actually trying to move. What is the opportunity, specifically?

Why it works: same instinct to help, but it asks for the specific opportunity before offering the relationship.
