Your Strategy

Your positioning

Positioning statement

Chris helps business operators find partners and plan how to work together. His experience includes cannabis.

The work includes understanding the opportunity, finding suitable partners, and agreeing on responsibilities after an introduction.

Category
Chris Boudreau | Business development and strategic partnerships
Distinctive idea
Partnerships with a clear business purpose
Founder role
Clients work directly with Chris.

The Focus Star

What your brand stands for

  1. Product Relationship-led growth and market development

    The offer combines relationship building, market foresight, business planning, and execution. Cannabis is a domain you know well, and the same approach carries into any market where follow-through decides the outcome.

  2. People Progressive operators seeking smarter growth

    Persona: The Progressive Growth Operator. Ambition is present. The missing piece is the relationships, the plan, or the follow-through that turns the opportunity into progress.

  3. Purpose Leave business better than you found it

    Each relationship should leave the other party in a meaningfully better position: heard first, then given a useful next step, then followed through.

  4. Promise Move the right relationships forward

    Brand DNA: Steadfast. Show up consistently, tell the hard truth with care, and stay when the work gets real.

  5. Personality Steady Hero

    Main Brand Archetype: Hero. Selfless, reliable, caring, analytical, and willing to carry responsibility without making it about himself.

Point of view

An introduction is useful when both businesses know why they are meeting.

Business approach

How you approach a partnership

Before the meeting Establish a business reason

Ask what each party wants from the relationship and what it can contribute.

During the discussion Clarify responsibilities

Identify who can decide, who will do the work, and what needs to be checked.

After the meeting Agree on the next action

Confirm what each party has agreed to do and when to follow up.

Brand archetype

Steady and responsible

Reliability is the central characteristic. Care shapes how you listen; analysis shapes the advice you give.

  1. Hero leads. Take responsibility for the work you agree to do.
  2. Caregiver and Sage support. Listen carefully and explain the reasoning behind your advice.
  3. Friend adds warmth. Be approachable and easy to talk to.

Keep the tone practical. Do not exaggerate your influence or promise an introduction before checking that it would be useful.

Voice principles

Clear advice, specific examples

01

Start with the business question

Name the decision the reader is trying to make.

What would you need this partner to do?
02

Explain your reasoning

Connect your advice to the circumstances.

I would clarify who can approve the project before arranging the meeting.
03

Use facts in examples

Say what was agreed and what happened next.

Describe the agreement, the responsibilities, and the outcome.
04

Make the next action specific

End with an action the recipient can respond to.

Could you send me a short description of the project before we speak?

Starter prompts

Prompts for common tasks

01

Write a LinkedIn post

Using the brand context above, write one LinkedIn post about evaluating a business partner. Explain one practical question an operator should ask and why. Under 150 words. Do not invent an example or claim a result.

02

Plan a month of posts

Using the brand context above, draft a four-week LinkedIn plan with two to three posts per week. Rotate the three content territories, give each post a one-line angle and a format (text post, short video, or document), and keep every angle inside the factual boundaries.

03

Draft the offer statement

Using the brand context above, write a short explanation of Chris's business development and partnership work for an operator considering a new market. Use the supplied offer. Do not add services, prices, credentials, or outcomes.

04

Shape a collaboration

Using the brand context above, write a short message proposing a joint perspective piece with an operator or firm already active in a market Chris knows. Explain what Chris brings, what the partner brings, and why it is useful to publish together. Plain, direct, no hard sell.

05

Build a campaign brief

Using the brand context above, draft three LinkedIn posts about evaluating a business partner. Each post should explain a different practical question. Use supplied facts only and do not invent client stories.

06

Prepare warm outreach

Using the brand context above, write a short message Chris can send to ten people he already knows who fit the audience or know someone who does. Name the opportunity in one sentence, ask one question, and make replying easy. Under 80 words.

Business development

Five ways to start a partnership conversation

  1. 01

    Direct outreach

    Ask an existing contact about a partnership need

    Your offer addresses an operator with a specific market opportunity. A conversation with someone you already know can identify that need.

    Do this now

    1. Contact an operator you know who has discussed expanding into a market. Ask about the partner they would need.

    Ready to use

    Message
    Is there a market you are trying to enter where finding a partner has been difficult? I am working on business development and partnerships, and would like to hear what is holding things up.

    Watch forA reply describing a specific market and the partner needed.

  2. 02

    Useful content

    Publish questions to ask before an introduction

    Your audience needs help judging a partnership. A short checklist demonstrates the questions behind your advice.

    Do this now

    1. Publish the checklist as a LinkedIn text post. Use it in conversations with operators evaluating a partner.

    Ready to use

    Checklist
    Before the introduction: What would make this worthwhile for each business? Who can make the decision? What would a small first project involve? What must you know before committing?

    Watch forAn operator brings you a partnership question or uses the checklist in a discussion.

  3. 03

    Collaboration

    Compare partnership questions with an operator

    A conversation with a cannabis operator connects your experience to a topic both audiences can use. The format makes your judgment visible.

    Do this now

    1. Invite a cannabis operator you already know to a short LinkedIn conversation. Agree on a date and the three questions each person will discuss.

    Ready to use

    Invitation
    Would you join me for a short LinkedIn conversation about evaluating a business partner? We could each bring three questions we ask before committing, then compare what would make us stop the discussion.

    Watch forThe operator agrees to participate and the conversation produces a relevant introduction or inquiry.

  4. 04

    Editorial outreach

    Pitch a practical partnership article

    Cannabis trade readers include operators considering partnerships. An article gives them practical questions to use when assessing a potential partner.

    Do this now

    1. Find the submissions contact of a cannabis business publication you read. Offer this article for its operator audience.

    Ready to use

    Article pitch
    I would like to contribute a practical article for cannabis operators: questions to ask before pursuing a business partnership. It would cover mutual benefit, decision authority, and the first project to test the relationship. Would that be useful for your readers?

    Watch forAn editor requests the article or an interview.

  5. 05

    Referrals

    Give contacts a clear description to pass on

    People who know you need a concise explanation of the work you want to be referred for. The message names the business situation.

    Do this now

    1. Share the description with a former collaborator who knows your work. Ask them to keep it for a relevant introduction.

    Ready to use

    Forwardable introduction
    I work with operators who need business partners for a new market or growth project. If someone you know is looking, I would be glad to hear what they are trying to do.

    Watch forA contact introduces an operator with a stated partnership or market-entry need.
