Your Strategy

Build from the inside out

Positioning statement

Frances builds website systems for museums and cultural nonprofits that staff can keep current after launch.

The work starts with the way exhibitions and programs are published. Frances designs the structure, trains the staff, and keeps the technical layer healthy through Goldlilys Media.

Category
Website systems for museums and cultural nonprofits
Distinctive idea
Built around the institution
Founder role
Frances is the brand. Goldlilys Media is the studio she runs. Content speaks as Frances, one person, with the institutions she supports.

The Focus Star

The whole brand in five lines

  1. Product A website their staff can keep alive for years

    The institution pays for a site that follows exhibitions, programs, and events, plus training so staff can publish without waiting on a developer.

  2. People Cultural institutions whose site must match the work

    Persona: The new director whose site lags the collection. A CEO, Executive Director, President, or Marketing Director at a museum or cultural nonprofit, facing an exhibition season or a leadership change, whose public site still looks quieter than the work on the floor.

  3. Purpose Give people hope and belonging through culture they can find

    Culture only travels if people can find it. The site is how a collection, a season, and a community stay visible to the next visitor.

  4. Promise Build from the inside out

    Brand DNA: Connection. The operating commitment is to build from the inside out, so the uniqueness can show and the connection can last.

  5. Personality Calm, human, and sure with technical complexity

    Curiosity, creativity, and connection, delivered as calm competence. The technical work gets translated so staff feel in control.

Point of view

A site that never changes reads as an inactive organization. Build from the inside out.

01The staff who live the season should be able to publish the season.

02Uniqueness lasts when the foundation fits how the institution actually works.

03Connection is the outcome. Inside-out is how it is kept.

Calm guidance through technical complexity

Personality

Calm, human, and sure with technical complexity.

A director should understand two things after a call: the site will reflect the institution, and staff will be able to publish without waiting for a developer.

In the roomCalm on the technical side

Explains the technical layer in language a museum team already uses. Staff leave knowing what they can update themselves.

On the pagePublishing system before surface styling

Lead with the way the team publishes. The visible design follows the structure of the institution's real work.

In the pictureLight, cared-for, and specific

Daylight in galleries and public rooms. Color that belongs to the institution. Black-slick portfolio chrome stays out, including as a first-screen field.

Brand archetype

A creator who makes complex systems usable

Creator leads because Frances builds an original system around each institution. Sage makes the technical layer understandable. Caregiver protects staff ownership. Magician supports the shift from a static brochure to an active site. Friend keeps the work personal.

  1. Creator leads. The site is an original built from the inside of the organization, so uniqueness can show and last.
  2. Sage and Caregiver carry it. Sage translates the technical work. Caregiver leaves staff able to update without waiting on a ticket.
  3. Magician supports the change. A brochure that never moves becomes a site that follows exhibitions, programs, and events.
  4. Friend stays a trace. Connection is the outcome. It is not the whole personality.

Outlaw, Hero, Lover, Jester, and Ruler stay at zero. Innocent and Explorer do too. Shock, theater, luxury-script chrome, and wanderlust do not belong here.

Voice principles

Sound like the person who leaves staff able to update

01

Name the living part

Talk about programs, exhibitions, events, and the people who publish them. A site that never changes reads as an inactive organization.

Every activity, every member supported, every volunteer should be on the website.
02

Translate the technical work

Say plugins, theme, and core in the same sentence as what the staff will actually do. Purely logical vendor language stays out.

I mainly update the plugins, themes, and core. The people updating contents are their museum staff.
03

Build from the inside out

Lead with the foundation that makes uniqueness last. Surface-only redesign is the near-miss.

Websites that are built with the right foundation from the beginning last, and what you see outward only shows well because the inside fits first.
04

Say who the work is not for

Political, financial, real estate, and companies that want to look like everyone else stay out. The niche is the standard.

I say no to anything political, financial, real estate, or what I call boring companies.

How it looks

Deep teal, open space, and daylight

Shell

Deep teal holds the first screen and cool paper carries long reading. Gold stays a small signal rather than a decorative finish.

Photography

Daylight in galleries and public rooms. Show the content systems that keep an institution current, without night-mode portfolio chrome.

Type

A clean geometric sans carries both headlines and reading copy. Scale and spacing create hierarchy without a heritage-brochure voice.

Leave out

Leave out aubergine brochure styling, black-slick chrome, ornamental frames, badge walls, and generic agency mockups.

Design decisions

Why it looks this way

Every choice below answers something the work already demands. The register it adds up to is modern, and luxurious.

01

The opening field

People
What is true

The new director decides in seconds, between meetings, whether something deserves real attention.

What the design does

Deep teal navy holds the first screen. It carries a single thought, and nothing else on the screen competes for that decision.

02

Headline type

Product
What is true

The work is finding the real point and leading with it.

What the design does

Futura carries headlines at one display size per screen. The page never runs two headlines arguing for the same attention.

03

Reading type

Product
What is true

Substance has to survive being read on a phone between two meetings.

What the design does

Futura carries body copy and navigation. It holds its shape at small sizes and stays readable in long stretches.

04

The accent

Promise
What is true

Attention is the scarcest thing in the rooms this work happens in.

What the design does

Gold takes the small details of the page: thin rules, numerals, an occasional emphasized word. It stays scarce enough to register every time it appears.

05

The reading field

Personality
What is true

Warmth is part of the work, and a screen of pure white has none of it.

What the design does

Cool paper carries body copy instead of a sterile white, so a full page of thinking stays easy to sit with.

06

Conviction

Personality
What is true

Warmth without softness around the truth means some points are not negotiable.

What the design does

Burnt clay marks a single point of conviction. It never runs as body text and never labels a control.

07

Photography

People
What is true

The new director is buying judgment from a person, in rooms where trust decides whether advice lands.

What the design does

Real photography of genuine engagement: across a table, mid-conversation, in rooms with texture and daylight, never a staged authority pose.

08

Space and density

Promise
What is true

A crowded page looks unsure which of its own claims to stand behind.

What the design does

Generous margins, hairline rules, and one thing to do per screen. No badge walls and no six claims stacked on the first view.
