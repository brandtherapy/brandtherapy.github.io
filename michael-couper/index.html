Your Strategy

Be the thinker who starts with the question

Positioning statement

Michael Couper helps independent thinkers see the whole picture before making a consequential choice, by connecting evidence, systems, art, and civic reality that a single discipline would miss.

Most public thinking picks a lane and stays there: the analyst's answer, the artist's take, the operator's certainty. Michael works across the lanes on purpose, because a consequential choice rarely respects one discipline. His authority comes from following the question until the fuller picture is visible.

Category
Cross-disciplinary judgment and public thinking
Distinctive idea
Follow the question
Founder role
Michael is the practice. The work he does across other ventures and institutions stays behind him.

The Focus Star

The brand in five lines

  1. Product The context and tools to see the whole picture.

    The product is the ability to move between disciplines, connect evidence that usually stays separated, and turn a wider field of view into a decision that actually holds.

  2. People Independent thinkers.

    Persona: The Independent Decision-Maker. Someone who resists premature certainty because the cost of a shallow judgment is real.

  3. Purpose To move society forward through better-informed human choices.

    Better information matters because choices have consequences. The goal is a fuller understanding that improves what people build, protect, and pass forward.

  4. Promise Follow the question.

    Brand DNA: Question First. Inquiry only matters when it changes the decision at the end of it, so the question gets followed before an answer gets defended.

  5. Personality A serious, playful polymath: rational, artistic, independent, with a mystical edge.

    Main Brand Archetype: Sage. The range is coherent. Analytical rigor, artistic imagination, dry playfulness, and a restrained mystical edge sit together as one character.

Point of view

The question that gets avoided is usually the one that would have changed the decision.

01A fuller picture costs more time than a faster answer, and it is the one that holds up later.

02Independent thinking means resisting certainty until the picture is actually complete.

03Evidence, inference, intuition, and opinion are different things. Naming which one is in use is part of the honesty.

Evidence

What backs up the position

The range Multiple disciplines, one method

The value is what becomes visible when evidence from different fields gets connected: technology, art, business, science, philosophy, and civic life read together.

The discipline Question the premise before the answer

Before accepting an answer, the premise gets examined, the missing perspective gets sought, and the evidence gets tested. The inquiry closes when it can support a real decision.

The honesty Evidence, inference, and opinion, named separately

A claim is only as strong as its type. What is sourced fact, what is interpretation, and what is personal judgment stay clearly marked, especially when the distinction changes how much weight the claim should carry.

The revision Willing to be wrong in public

A public position that updates when the evidence changes is inquiry working as intended.

None of this replaces expertise. It is what expertise looks like when it stays honest about what it does not yet know.

Brand archetype

The sage who follows the question

Thirteen words define the brand: analytical, artistic, calm, curious, honest, independent, insightful, knowledgeable, mature, original, serious, thoughtful, trustworthy. The mix below follows from them.

  1. Sage leads. Context and evidence before judgment. This is the position itself.
  2. Creator carries it. Frameworks and tools that turn a wider field of evidence into something usable.
  3. Innocent supports. Honesty and clarity that keep the inquiry from turning into spin or shock.
  4. Explorer, Friend, Hero, Ruler, Outlaw, Magician, and Caregiver stay traces. Independence, approachability, resolve, standards, a restrained mystical edge, and generosity, present but never the lead.

Stay off guru staging, debate-personality framing, and salesman cadence. No borrowed certainty, no contrarianism for attention, no mystical language without a real question behind it.

Voice principles

Sound like someone who actually followed the question

01

Question before conclusion

Ask what problem is actually being solved before defending an answer.

Before we argue about the answer, what question are we actually trying to solve?
02

Hold the competing view

Bring in the perspective that complicates the easy answer, and keep it in view until the decision is made.

The data matters. So does the context the data cannot hold.
03

Name what kind of claim it is

Evidence, inference, intuition, and opinion are not the same thing. Say which one is being used.

That part is the evidence. This next part is just my read on it.
04

Serious does not mean heavy

Keep real ideas in plain, direct language, and treat revision as a strength.

Changing your mind is not weakness. It is evidence that the inquiry worked.

How it looks

Working contexts, exact wardrobe, no performance

Presence

A direct eye line, present and engaged, never performance intensity. Working posture at a desk, teaching, or in conversation, with real evidence visible in frame.

Wardrobe

A dark fine-gauge turtleneck with an unstructured wool blazer by default. A fitted polo for casual or media settings, a minimalist tuxedo for evening. Exact and quiet, never corporate shine.

Behavior

Caught in an observable act of inquiry: notes and evidence at a desk, teaching a student, speaking into a mic. Never standing still just to display an outfit.

Not this

No guru staging, no stage-performer spotlight, no professor costume, no arms-crossed dominance. The existing visual system stays as it is; this Sprint does not touch it.

Starter prompts

Six prompts to start from

01

LinkedIn post

Write a short post that opens with a real, consequential question. Name the premise most people accept without checking. Show what changes once one more discipline or one more piece of evidence enters the picture. Close with what actually became clearer. First person, direct sentences, dry wit welcome, no listicle format, no company names.

02

Month plan

Plan four weeks of posts that follow one consequential question from the first uncertain framing to a landed decision. Week 1 states the position. Week 2 shows the method on a real example. Week 3 brings in a competing perspective fairly. Week 4 opens an invitation to talk. No company names, no invented results.

03

Written framework

Turn one recurring question into a short, reusable framework: three to five questions someone can ask themselves before a consequential choice. No jargon, no diagrams that require a key. Grounded in the same follow-the-question method, giving the reader the tool rather than the answer.

04

Collaboration brief

Draft a short brief for a public conversation with someone from an adjacent field. Name the one real question neither side has fully answered, why it matters now, and what a useful disagreement between the two of them would sound like. No company names, no assumed outcome.

05

Campaign brief

Draft a short campaign brief for saying the core position once, clearly, across formats: one long-form piece, one short video cut, one pinned post. State the one idea the whole campaign stands on in plain language, and the one signal that would show it landed.

06

Warm outreach brief

Draft a short, personal note to someone already in his orbit, pointing at the new public position and asking one honest question: does this land, and what would make it more useful. No pricing, no pitch, one clear ask.
