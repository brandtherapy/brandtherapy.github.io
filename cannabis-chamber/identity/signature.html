02
Source-backed behavior

Raster reference

The inspected raster board is the source for behavior. Notes below name the use cases without replacing the source image with coded demos.

Color field · light surfaces

Color field

Forest, teal and coral arcs to brass nodes on ivory or white, the default. Master images/signature/device-field-color.png. Place it over-scaled so the lines bleed off every edge; opacity is the dial, full strength as art, lower as an atmospheric field.

Monochrome field · dark surfaces

Monochrome field

A single-ink knockout of the same geometry. Masters images/signature/device-mono-ivory.png (line) and device-field-mono.png (bleed field). On dark surfaces it sits tone-on-tone at about 8 to 12 percent, the same green a shade lifted, never a loud two-color graphic. This is the reversed treatment for forest, ink, busy, or photographic grounds.

The bleed rule · system law

The bleed rule

The field is a connective surface, not a placed logo. It always runs off every edge of the frame, and the lines never dead-end inside the artwork. Over-scale and crop so loose tips fall outside the frame, and keep a clear margin for the lockup and copy.

03
Recreation · Rules

Non-negotiable geometry

The strict rules for rebuilding the signature across SVG, raster, motion, layout, and pattern use.

Construction law

No visible line endings

Every visible arc must leave the live frame. A stroke cap may exist only outside the crop, mask, artboard, or container. In SVG, exposed endpoints must clear the viewBox by stroke radius plus 8 units unless fully covered by a node knockout.

Construction law

Artboard is a crop window

The field is drawn larger than the surface, then cropped. A visible border may frame the crop, but the lines continue beyond it. Do not stop an arc at the border or in the middle of the composition.

Construction law

Geometric SVG only

Vector recreation uses deliberate SVG arcs and circles from approved construction geometry. Do not auto-trace a PNG, clean up a raster vectorization, or hand-wobble the paths.

Construction law

Node knockout order

Draw arcs first, draw field-color knockout circles around every node second, then draw the brass node circles on top. The knockout ring is mandatory separation, not a decoration.

Construction law

Scale before simplifying

If a small format makes node rings or arc spacing collapse, scale the full field larger and crop harder. Do not remove the bleed law, clip a line end into view, or let arcs visually touch brass nodes.

Medium recipe

Hero, poster, backdrop

Oversize the field beyond the final surface, then crop. Every visible arc must feel like it continues past at least one frame edge.

Medium recipe

Cards, social, slides

Use tighter crops only when all loose tips remain outside the frame. The signature is never a centered emblem or a closed icon.

Medium recipe

Motion

Extend arcs beyond the motion frame before panning, masking, or revealing. Motion may reveal more curve, but it must never reveal a terminal.

Medium recipe

Dark or photographic surfaces

Use the mono treatment at 8 to 12 percent unless the field is the main art moment. The same crop, bleed, and node-separation laws apply.

Medium recipe

Pattern and repeat

Use only prepared tiles whose edges cut through continuing arcs. A repeating tile may never expose a stroke cap inside the pattern.

Recognition test

If someone sees sweeping arcs converging at brass nodes and running off every edge of a surface, they should feel the Chamber's common ground before reading a word.

04
Construction · Spec

Build rules

The construction reference. How to draw it, weight it, place it.

The Common Ground Field: signature device spec

The Common Ground Field is the Cannabis Chamber signature device. It is not the logo, and it must not replace the logo. The logo names the brand. The signature creates the feeling of common ground through arcs that converge at brass nodes and continue beyond the frame.

The mark grammar (bearings 270, 30, and 150 degrees; forest, teal, and coral arcs to a brass node) is the reference for the convergence. The color field is the default treatment; the dark-surface field is the same geometry recolored to a single ink.

Construction

The field inherits the D2 mark geometry:

  • Center node: the mark's brass dot radius
  • Module bearings: 270, 30, and 150 degrees
  • Line colors: forest, teal, and coral
  • Node color: brass
  • Motion: curved convergence toward the node, never loose decoration
  • Frame behavior: every line bleeds off the frame edge

The device is built from the same converging geometry as the mark, then tiled or over-scaled into a wider surface. The signature is recognizable by the convergence behavior, not by showing the full mark.

Non-negotiable reconstruction rules

  • No visible line endings: every arc must leave the live frame. A stroke cap may exist only outside the crop, mask, artboard, or container. The viewer may see a line cut by the frame edge; the viewer must never see a rounded or blunt terminal sitting inside the artwork.
  • Clean geometric source: do not use bitmap tracing, auto-vectorization, or hand-wobbled Bezier cleanup. SVG work is built from deliberate arcs and circles, using the logo construction as the geometry language.
  • Draw order: draw arcs first, then field-color knockout discs around every node, then brass node discs on top. The knockout is what keeps the brass node separated from the crossing arcs.
  • Node separation: each brass node needs a visible field-color ring around it at final output size. If the ring collapses at small sizes, scale the whole field up or simplify the crop; do not let arcs visually touch the node.
  • Frame behavior: the artboard or container is a crop window, not the outer edge of the drawing. Lines extend beyond it and are hidden by the crop.
  • SVG endpoint clearance: every exposed SVG arc endpoint must sit outside the viewBox by at least its stroke radius plus 8 construction units. The only allowed internal endpoint is one fully covered by a field-color node knockout.

Masters

The signature is maintained as a curated set of locked master files:

  • Color field for ivory or white surfaces
  • Dark-surface field treatment
  • Single ivory line device
  • Transparent field for controlled compositing
  • Color and dark-surface reference crops
  • Geometric SVG recreation, when needed, must be produced from approved construction geometry. It must not be produced by tracing a raster master.
  • Web integration source: images/signature/common-ground-field-geometric.svg. This is the vector-only page asset: six deliberate arc paths, six field-color node knockouts, and six brass nodes.

Medium recipes

  • Hero, poster, and backdrop: scale the field larger than the surface, then crop it. At least one side of every visible arc exits through a frame edge.
  • Cards, social, and slides: use a tighter crop only if every arc still feels like it continues beyond the frame. Never center the field as a closed icon.
  • Motion: pan, reveal, or mask the field only after the arcs have already been extended beyond the motion frame. A moving crop may reveal more arc, but it must never reveal a terminal.
  • Dark or photographic surfaces: use the mono treatment at 8 to 12 percent unless it is the primary art moment. The same bleed and node-separation rules apply.
  • Pattern or repeat use: repeat only from a prepared tile whose edges cut through continuing arcs. No tile may expose a cap inside the repeat.

Usage

  • Use the color field on ivory or white.
  • Use the mono field on forest, ink, photography, or busy surfaces.
  • Over-scale the field so line endings fall outside the frame.
  • Keep content and logo clear zones above the field.
  • Treat opacity as the dial: full strength for art moments, 8 to 12 percent for dark atmospheric surfaces.

Misuse

  • Do not use the signature as the logo.
  • Do not place the field as a centered icon.
  • Do not let line endings stop inside the frame.
  • Do not recolor the field outside the locked color, mono, and inverse system.
  • Do not add glow, gradients, shadows, or plant motifs.
  • Do not redraw the field or its arcs by hand. Use locked raster masters for raster use and approved geometric construction for SVG/vector use.
