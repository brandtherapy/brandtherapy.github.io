Look & Feel

The operational layer of the visual identity. Color, typography, layout, photography, and the categories the brand refuses to look like.

01
Color system

Palette

02
Type system

Typography

CCC type system: Outfit carries editorial warmth and major brand headlines; Public Sans carries navigation, body text, labels, forms, and practical service detail. The renderer must not fall back to FP typography.

Editorial display
Outfit

Use: brand headlines, expressive lines, source-board phrases, footer promise, and warm high-touch moments.

Editorial echo
Outfit

Use: mixed-headline emphasis and large pull moments. Keep it elegant and spacious, not ornate.

Functional sans
Public Sans

Use: body text, form fields, small proof lines, and practical advisory copy. Keep it plain, legible, and human.

03
System · Rules

Do & Don't

Non-negotiable. These rules exist to maintain visual coherence across every touchpoint.

Do

Lead with whitespace on ivory or white

Do

Use the constructed lockups for every public-facing name treatment

Do

Hold the master mark down to 32 px; the favicon renders it at 48 / 32 / 24 px (no separate small variant)

Do

Bold the load-bearing phrases (2-4 per long-form section)

Do

Use sharp 90-degree corners on every layout element

Do

Photograph members in serious business settings (boardrooms, conferences, facilities)

Don't

Use em-dashes (U+2014) or en-dashes (U+2013) in public paragraph copy

Don't

Round corners or echo the mark's circles as decorative UI bubbles

Don't

Introduce a third typeface

Don't

Recolor, rotate, mirror, or restroke the mark; never rebuild it from a font

Don't

Place the color mark on coral, teal, brass, or photographic backgrounds

Don't

Set "CCC" as a public wordmark or headline brand name

Don't

Use cannabis-leaf graphics as a brand signal

Don't

Use stoner aesthetics, neon, or hype graphics

Don't

Use dark canvas as default

04
Imagery · Photography

Photography

How real photos and AI-generated imagery should look.

Style: Industrial minimalism. Boardrooms, conference floors, member-led panel discussions, facility scale, product as artifact (not consumer SKU shots). Editorial photojournalism over staged marketing.

Tone: Serious, premium, business-first. Subjects are operators at work, not consumers. Lighting is natural or directional studio; never moody nightclub or warm bar-lit.

Modes:

  • Convening mode: people in serious conversation. Conference rooms, panels, two-person dialogues. Hands gesturing toward outcomes.
  • Facility mode: scale, infrastructure, operations. Production floors, distribution centers, regulatory environments.
  • Standards mode: abstract structural imagery: patterns, geometric forms, architectural lines. Used for thematic section breaks.

Forbidden imagery:

  • Cannabis-leaf graphics or imagery as primary visual
  • Stoner aesthetic (smoke, hippie cues, tie-dye, hands rolling joints)
  • Consumer cannabis product shots (vape pens on display, edibles as candy, etc.)
  • Lifestyle/party photography
  • Stock-photo group selfies, "happy diverse team" cliches
  • Warm gradient backdrops
  • "Happy plant" / "growing plant" imagery as visual metaphor

Editorial default: light color overall, restrained accent, business documentary register.

05
Categories the brand refuses

Anti-references

What this brand is visually and categorically NOT, framed as anti-targets for AI image generation, layout work, and brand positioning.

Consumer cannabis brand aesthetics

Bright greens, leaf graphics, smoke effects, party scenes, dispensary product photography. The Chamber serves business operators, not consumers.

Stoner culture aesthetics

Tie-dye, peace signs, hippie typography, smoke iconography, 420 references. The Chamber is institutional, not subcultural.

Cannabis lobby/advocacy aesthetics (old guard)

Earnest activism graphics, hand-drawn protest energy, "legalize" iconography. The Chamber is a business body, not an activism group.

Networking-event aesthetics

Cocktail glasses clinking, neon "MIXER" overlays, "swipe right for cannabis" tropes. The Chamber convenes serious work, not parties.

Generic trade-association aesthetics

Stiff corporate stock photography, gavel-and-flag iconography, "Established 1923" badges. The Chamber is modern and operator-led, not stuffy.

Wellness/healing cannabis aesthetics

Soft pastels, leaf-as-medicine, "your wellness journey." The Chamber is business, not therapy.
