P2 / People

Founders and C-suite of small to mid-size cannabis businesses

Adam serves founders and C-suite leaders of small to mid-size cannabis operations who want to raise their own bar and pull the rest of the ecosystem up with them. They believe the most resilient companies align values, products, and numbers.

On this page
  1. What it means
  2. Do and avoid
  3. Put it to work

01

What it means

The right client is not the operator who needs to be convinced that data matters. The right client is the one who already knows something is wrong with the numbers and wants to fix it for real.

02

Do and avoid

Do

  • Founders and C-suite of cannabis operations: retail, cultivation, manufacturing.
  • Small to mid-size businesses: founder-led or tightly-held, not publicly traded at scale.
  • Operators who want long-term resilience over short-term optics.
  • Leaders who believe honest numbers and strong values are the same decision.
  • Multi-license and multi-site operations where data silos have already caused problems.

Avoid

  • Operators who want to game metrics rather than fix them.
  • Companies seeking data work to pass a single audit without changing the underlying system.
  • Large corporate cannabis that has a dedicated data team and does not need an outside independent voice.
  • Consumer-facing cannabis brands not connected to operations data.
  • Operators who prioritize marketing optics over internal accuracy.

03

Put it to work

  • Strategy: qualify clients on willingness to act on honest findings, not on company size alone.
  • Copy: address the person who already suspects the data is wrong. Do not sell the need for data.
  • Visuals: speak to serious operators, never consumers, using the Direction 2 identity.
  • Sales: the right client will recognize themselves in the problem statement.
  • Content: write for the operator who reads industry reports critically, not the operator who shares them.
