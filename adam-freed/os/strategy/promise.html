P4 / Promise

Data Integrity

Data Integrity is the brand promise. Adam brings data alignment, visibility, and reliability to the point where operators can trust the numbers guiding the business. He will not dress up bad data or bend findings to a preferred conclusion.

On this page
  1. What it means
  2. Do and avoid
  3. Put it to work

01

What it means

The standard starts with the data: alignment before reporting, visibility before story, integrity before action.

02

Do and avoid

Do

  • Primary promise: Data Integrity.
  • Supporting lines: The standard starts with the data. Numbers that check out. Honest from seed to sale.
  • Independent advisory as the structural proof of the promise: no vendor commissions, no platform bias.
  • Findings delivered as found, not as desired.
  • Engagements that begin only when the client is ready for honest results.

Avoid

  • Any tagline or copy that softens the independence of findings.
  • Data work contingent on a predetermined conclusion.
  • Platform or vendor language that implies bias.
  • Promises of specific business outcomes from honest data (the data delivers the finding; the operator acts on it).
  • Generic advisory language: insights, transformation, unlock.

03

Put it to work

  • Strategy: use Data Integrity as the decision filter. If the work requires softening a finding, it does not belong in an Adam Freed engagement.
  • Copy: short declarative sentences. The promise is a statement, not a pitch.
  • Visuals: use The Connection Cable as the Promise-derived signature. One cable links systems, evidence, photography, typography, and applications.
  • Sales: the promise pre-qualifies clients who want real findings.
  • Content: write from the refusal. State the standard once, then back it with evidence.
